# MASTER COMPENDIUM: Fall-Risk-Increasing Drugs (FRIDs) & Deprescribing Guidelines (2025–2026)

**Clinical References:**
* *Deprescribing in Older People: A clinical practice guideline* (16 September 2025) — WACHA, Centre for Optimisation of Medicines, University of Western Australia (Endorsed by RACGP, ANZSGM, PSA, AdPha, ADEA).
* *Indian Geriatric Pharmacotherapy & Clinical Practice Standards (2026)* — Aligned with ICMR Guidelines, AIIMS Department of Geriatric Medicine, National Programme for Health Care of the Elderly (NPHCE), and CDSCO / NLEM Directives.
* *International Fall Prevention Framework* — World Guidelines for Falls Prevention and Management, and the European STOPPFall (EuGMS) Consensus.

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Master Drug Counts

In older adults ($\ge 65$ years, and $\ge 50$ years for vulnerable populations), medication-induced falls represent the leading cause of preventable hospitalisation, traumatic brain injury, and hip fractures. 

While classical geriatric screening tools historically focused solely on immediate sedatives, modern 2025–2026 guidelines recognize that **both direct and indirect (hidden) mechanisms** destabilize an older adult's balance, gait, postural tone, and bone integrity.

### Grand Totals at a Glance

$$\mathbf{25\text{ Pharmacological FRID Classes}} \quad\mathbf{\Big/}\quad \mathbf{174\text{ Individual Generic Medications}}$$

* **Part A: Direct / Classical FRIDs (16 Classes — 147 Drugs):** Medications causing acute sedation, psychomotor slowing, orthostatic hypotension, bradyarrhythmias, or acute hypoglycaemia.
* **Part B: "Hidden" & Indirect FRIDs (9 Classes — 27 Drugs):** Medications causing insidious muscle wasting (sarcopenia), peripheral neuropathy (sensory ataxia), electrolyte-induced flaccidity (hypomagnesaemia/hypokalaemia), steroid myopathy, or systemic beta-blockade from eye drops.

## 3. PART A: Detailed Listing of Direct FRIDs (147 Drugs)

---

### Class 1: Antihypertensives (Non-Diuretic) — [20 Drugs]
* **ACE Inhibitors (8):** Captopril, Enalapril (*Envas*), Fosinopril, Lisinopril (*Lipril*), Perindopril (*Coversyl*), Quinapril, Ramipril (*Cardace*), Trandolapril
* **Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers (ARBs) (5):** Candesartan (*Candar*), Irbesartan, Olmesartan (*Omesar*), Telmisartan (*Telma*), Valsartan (*Valzaar*)
* **ARNI (1):** Sacubitril + Valsartan (*Vymada, Cidmus*)
* **Dihydropyridine CCBs (Vascular) (4):** Amlodipine (*Stamlo*), Cilnidipine (*Cilacar*), Felodipine (*Felogard*), Nifedipine (*Nicardia*)
* **Non-Dihydropyridine CCBs (Cardiac) (2):** Diltiazem (*Dilzem*), Verapamil (*Calaptin*)
* **Fall Mechanism:** Impairs baroreceptor reflex; induces orthostatic and postprandial hypotension.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Deprescribe if $\text{SBP} < 150\text{ mmHg}$ (age $\ge 80$) or $< 140\text{ mmHg}$ (age 75–79) with recurrent falls. Restart if $\text{SBP} \ge 150\text{ mmHg}$ on 3 consecutive visits; restart immediately if $\text{SBP} \ge 180\text{ mmHg}$.

---

### Class 2: Centrally Acting Antihypertensives & Direct Vasodilators — [5 Drugs]
* **Centrally Acting (3):** Clonidine (*Arkamin*), Methyldopa (*Emdopa*), Moxonidine (*Moxovas*)
* **Direct Arteriolar Vasodilators (2):** Hydralazine, Minoxidil
* **Fall Mechanism:** Centrally blunts sympathetic outflow causing postural pooling and daytime somnolence.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Priority #1 to stop among antihypertensives. Halve dose every 2 weeks to avoid rebound hypertensive encephalopathy.

---

### Class 3: Diuretics — [9 Drugs]
* **Loop Diuretics (2):** Furosemide (*Lasix*), Torsemide / Torasemide (*Dytor, Tide*)
* **Thiazide Diuretics (1):** Hydrochlorothiazide (*Aquazide*)
* **Thiazide-Like Diuretics (2):** Chlortalidone (*Hythalton*), Indapamide (*Natrilix*)
* **Aldosterone Antagonists / K-Sparing (3):** Spironolactone (*Aldactone*), Eplerenone (*Eptus*), Finerenone (*Kerendia*)
* **ENaC Blocker (1):** Amiloride
* **Fall Mechanism:** Dehydration, intravascular hypovolaemia, severe hyponatraemia, nocturia rushing.
* **Furosemide Taper:** $<40\text{ mg/day} \rightarrow$ stop directly. $40\text{ mg/day} \rightarrow 20\text{ mg}$ for 1 week, then stop. $80\text{ mg/day} \rightarrow 40\text{ mg}$ for 2 weeks, then stop.

---

### Class 4: Beta-Adrenoceptor Blockers — [7 Drugs]
1. Metoprolol (*Betaloc, Prolomet*)
2. Atenolol (*Aten*)
3. Bisoprolol (*Concor*)
4. Carvedilol (*Carca*)
5. Nebivolol (*Nebicard*)
6. Propranolol (*Ciplar*)
7. Labetalol (*Labebet*)
* **Fall Mechanism:** Symptomatic bradycardia ($<60\text{ bpm}$), AV node block, blunted compensatory reflex during standing, muscle fatigue.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Taper by 50% every 2 weeks. Never stop abruptly due to rebound tachycardia and ischaemia.

---

### Class 5: Organic Nitrates — [3 Drugs]
1. Glyceryl trinitrate (GTN) (*Nitrocontin, Angispan*)
2. Isosorbide mononitrate (*Monotrate*)
3. Isosorbide dinitrate (*Sorbitrate*)
* **Fall Mechanism:** Potent systemic venodilation causes massive venous pooling and orthostatic syncope.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Taper long-acting formulations if angina-free for $\ge 6$ months; keep sublingual GTN available for PRN rescue.

---

### Class 6: Antiarrhythmics & Cardiac Glycosides — [3 Drugs]
1. Digoxin (*Lanoxin*)
2. Sotalol (*Sotagard*)
3. Amiodarone (*Cordarone*)
* **Fall Mechanism:** Declining renal clearance leads to drug accumulation, bradyarrhythmias, heart block, dizziness, and visual halos.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Deprescribe digoxin in HFrEF if in sinus rhythm and on GDMT. Deprescribe sotalol if AF is permanent.

---

### Class 7: Urological & Peripheral Alpha-1 Blockers — [4 Drugs]
1. Tamsulosin (*Urimax*)
2. Silodosin (*Silodal*)
3. Alfuzosin (*Alfusin*)
4. Prazosin (*Minipress-XL*)
* **Fall Mechanism:** Peripheral arteriolar dilation causes first-dose syncope and severe orthostatic dizziness.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Cease post-TURP or if causing orthostatic symptoms. Discontinue without tapering.

---

### Class 8: Antipsychotics (Neuroleptics) — [19 Drugs]
1. Olanzapine (*Oleanz*)
2. Quetiapine (*Seroquin*)
3. Risperidone (*Sizodon*)
4. Haloperidol (*Serenace*)
5. Aripiprazole (*Arip MT*)
6. Chlorpromazine (*Largactil*)
7. Amisulpride (*Sulpitac*)
8. Clozapine (*Sizopin*)
9. Trifluoperazine (*Neocalm*)
10. Flupentixol (*Fluanxol*)
11. Paliperidone (*Paliris*)
12. Zuclopenthixol (*Clopixol*)
13. Asenapine
14. Brexpiprazole
15. Cariprazine
16. Droperidol
17. Lurasidone
18. Periciazine
19. Ziprasidone
* **Fall Mechanism:** Heavy sedation, alpha-adrenergic blockade (postural hypotension), and dopamine D2 antagonism causing Parkinsonian gait rigidity and ataxia.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Review BPSD at 12 weeks; cease if stable. Taper by 25% every 1–4 weeks, or use hyperbolic micro-tapering (5–10% monthly) to prevent withdrawal dyskinesia.

---

### Class 9: Benzodiazepine Anxiolytics — [6 Drugs]
1. Alprazolam (*Alprax, Restyl*)
2. Clonazepam (*Zapiz, Lonazep*)
3. Diazepam (*Calmpose, Valium*)
4. Lorazepam (*Ativan*)
5. Clobazam (*Frisium*)
6. Oxazepam (*Serepax*)
* **Fall Mechanism:** Allosteric GABA-A activation causes psychomotor slowing, ataxia, and loss of postural muscle tone.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Deprescribe all use $>4$ weeks. Taper by 25% every 1–4 weeks.

---

### Class 10: Sedative-Hypnotics & Z-Drugs — [6 Drugs]
* **Hypnotic Benzodiazepines (4):** Flunitrazepam, Midazolam, Nitrazepam (*Nitrosun*), Temazepam
* **Z-Drugs (2):** Zolpidem (*Nitrest*), Zopiclone (*Zopicon*)
* **Fall Mechanism:** Residual morning sedation, nocturnal disorientation, ataxia during nighttime bathroom visits.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Discontinue chronic use ($>4$ weeks); taper by 25% every 1–4 weeks. Transition to sleep hygiene and CBT-I.

---

### Class 11: Antidepressants — [23 Drugs]
* **TCAs (6):** Amitriptyline (*Tryptomer*), Clomipramine, Dosulepin (*Prothiaden*), Doxepin, Imipramine, Nortriptyline (*Sensival*)
* **SSRIs (6):** Citalopram, Escitalopram (*Nexito*), Fluoxetine (*Flunil*), Fluvoxamine, Paroxetine (*Pexep*), Sertraline (*Zosert*)
* **SNRIs (3):** Desvenlafaxine (*D-Veniz*), Duloxetine (*Duzela*), Venlafaxine (*Venlor*)
* **MAOIs (3):** Phenelzine, Tranylcypromine, Moclobemide
* **Atypicals / Others (5):** Agomelatine, Mianserin, Mirtazapine (*Mirtaz*), Reboxetine, Vortioxetine
* **Fall Mechanism:** TCAs cause severe orthostasis; SSRIs/SNRIs induce SIADH hyponatraemia, sedation, and motor restlessness.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Deprescribe after 6–12 months of remission from major depression. Taper by 25% every 1–4 weeks.

---

### Class 12: Opioid Analgesics — [12 Drugs]
1. Tramadol (*Tramazac, Ultracet*)
2. Morphine (*Morphitroy*)
3. Buprenorphine (*Norspan patches*)
4. Codeine *(pain combos & cough syrups)*
5. Fentanyl (*Durogesic*)
6. Tapentadol (*Tepenta*)
7. Methadone
8. Hydromorphone
9. Oxycodone
10. Alfentanil
11. Remifentanil
12. Pethidine
* **Fall Mechanism:** Profound CNS sedation, dizziness, cognitive blunting, respiratory depression.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Recurrent falls is an absolute contraindication to continuing chronic opioids for non-cancer pain. Taper by 5–10% MED weekly (if used $<12\text{m}$) or monthly (if used $\ge 12\text{m}$).

---

### Class 13: Gabapentinoids — [2 Drugs]
1. Pregabalin (*Pregalin, Maxgalin*)
2. Gabapentin (*Gabapin*)
* **Fall Mechanism:** Voltage-gated calcium channel modulation causes somnolence, dizziness, and cerebellar gait ataxia.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Deprescribe if no objective relief after 2–3 months trial for neuropathic pain. Taper by 25% weekly.

---

### Class 14: Antiepileptics / Anticonvulsants — [20 Drugs]
1. Sodium Valproate (*Encorate*)
2. Phenytoin (*Eptoin*)
3. Carbamazepine (*Tegrital*)
4. Oxcarbazepine (*Oxetol*)
5. Levetiracetam (*Levipil*)
6. Lamotrigine (*Lamitor*)
7. Lacosamide (*Lacoset*)
8. Phenobarbital (*Gardenal*)
9. Primidone
10. Ethosuximide
11. Brivaracetam
12. Cannabidiol
13. Perampanel
14. Rufinamide
15. Stiripentol
16. Sulthiame
17. Tiagabine
18. Topiramate (*Topamac*)
19. Vigabatrin
20. Zonisamide
* **Fall Mechanism:** Suppression of neuronal conduction leads to sedation, diplopia, vestibular dysfunction, and delayed balance reflexes.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Deprescribe non-epilepsy indications if ineffective (taper by 25% weekly). In epilepsy, consider only after $\ge 2$ seizure-free years under neurologist care (taper 10–25% monthly; no driving permitted).

---

### Class 15: Sedating Antiemetics / Vestibular Sedatives — [4 Drugs]
1. Prochlorperazine (*Stemetil*)
2. Cinnarizine (*Stugeron*)
3. Flunarizine (*Sibelium*)
4. Metoclopramide (*Reglan*)
* **Fall Mechanism:** Dopamine D2 and histaminergic blockade causes sedation, hypotension, drug-induced parkinsonism, and extrapyramidal ataxia.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Limit to acute vertigo ($<5$ days). Discontinue long-term use; contraindicated in Parkinson's disease.

---

### Class 16: Genitourinary Anticholinergics — [5 Drugs]
1. Flavoxate (*Urispas*)
2. Oxybutynin (*Ditropan*)
3. Tolterodine (*Roliten*)
4. Solifenacin (*Solicept*)
5. Darifenacin (*Dariten*)
* **Fall Mechanism:** High anticholinergic burden causes delirium, sedation, accommodation paresis (blurred vision), and confusion.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Offer deprescribing to all older adults with high fall risk or cognitive impairment. Cease directly without tapering.

---

### Class 17: Hypoglycaemic Diabetes Agents — [6 Drugs]
* **Sulfonylureas (4):** Glimepiride (*Amaryl*), Gliclazide (*Diamicron*), Glibenclamide (*Daonil*), Glipizide (*Glynase*)
* **Insulins (2 main groups):** Premixed Human Insulin 30/70 (*Human Mixtard*), Basal/Rapid Analogues (*Lantus, Novorapid*)
* **Fall Mechanism:** Neuroglycopenic hypoglycaemia ($\text{glucose} < 4.0\text{ mmol/L}$) causes dizziness, transient focal weakness, confusion, and syncope.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Prioritise stopping insulin first, then sulfonylureas, when $\text{HbA}_{1c}$ is below target ($<7.0\text{--}7.5\%$).

---

### Class 18: Dopaminergic Anti-Parkinson Agents — [3 Drugs]
1. Levodopa + Carbidopa (*Syndopa*)
2. Pramipexole (*Pramipex*)
3. Ropinirole (*Ropark*)
* **Fall Mechanism:** Induces severe orthostatic hypotension in $>50\%$ of users, along with motor dyskinesias and sudden daytime sleepiness.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Reassess dose if orthostasis or hallucinations occur. Taper levodopa by 100 mg every 2 weeks to avoid acute akinetic crisis.

## 4. PART B: Detailed Listing of "Hidden" & Indirect FRIDs (27 Drugs)

---

### Class 19: Proton-Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) — [5 Drugs]
1. Pantoprazole (*Pan, Pantocid*)
2. Rabeprazole (*Razo, Rabekind*)
3. Omeprazole (*Omez*)
4. Esomeprazole (*Nexpro*)
5. Lansoprazole (*Lanzol*)
* **Hidden Fall & Fracture Mechanism:**
  1. **Hypomagnesaemia & Hypocalcaemia:** Causes muscle tremors, cramps, tetany, and gait weakness.
  2. **Vitamin B12 Malabsorption:** Leads to subacute combined degeneration, peripheral neuropathy, and sensory ataxia (staggering gait).
  3. **Accelerated Osteoporosis:** Impairs calcium absorption, causing low-trauma hip and femur fractures.
  4. **The Indian FDC Danger:** Fixed-dose combinations with **Levosulpiride** (*Pan-L*) cause severe **Drug-Induced Parkinsonism (tremors, shuffling gait, falls)**.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Deprescribe after 8–12 weeks if no bleeding ulcer or Barrett's. Halve dose every 2 weeks, switch to alternate days or PRN antacids. Monitor for rebound acid hypersecretion (peaks at 4–8 weeks).

---

### Class 20: SGLT2 Inhibitors ("Gliflozins") — [3 Drugs]
1. Dapagliflozin (*Forxiga, Oxra*)
2. Empagliflozin (*Jardiance*)
3. Canagliflozin (*Invokana*)
* **Hidden Fall Mechanism:** Osmotic diuresis causes volume contraction and postural hypotension. In frail elderly (BMI $<22$), it induces loss of skeletal muscle mass (sarcopenia), destabilizing balance.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Deprescribe if causing recurrent orthostatic dizziness, dehydration, or progressive muscle wasting.

---

### Class 21: Skeletal Muscle Relaxants — [4 Drugs]
1. Thiocolchicoside (*Myoril, Thioquest*)
2. Tizanidine (*Sirdalud*)
3. Baclofen (*Lioresal*)
4. Chlorzoxazone (*in Flexon-MR, Mobizox*)
* **Hidden Fall Mechanism:** Profound skeletal hypotonia (loss of tone in thigh and calf muscles). Older adults lose the reflex muscle strength required to recover from a minor stumble.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Never use long-term in older adults. Discontinue immediately or taper over 3–5 days.

---

### Class 22: Systemic Corticosteroids — [3 Drugs]
1. Prednisolone (*Wysolone*)
2. Methylprednisolone (*Medrol*)
3. Dexamethasone (*Dexona*)
* **Hidden Fall Mechanism:** **Steroid Myopathy** (selective atrophy of pelvic girdle and thigh muscles). Patients struggle to stand up from chairs or Indian-style toilets and collapse.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Taper by 10–20% weekly. When approaching physiological replacement ($5\text{ mg}$ prednisolone), taper by **1 mg every 4–8 weeks** to prevent adrenal crisis.

---

### Class 23: GLP-1 Receptor Agonists — [3 Drugs]
1. Semaglutide (*Ozempic, Rybelsus*)
2. Liraglutide (*Victoza*)
3. Dulaglutide (*Trulicity*)
* **Hidden Fall Mechanism:** In patients $>70$ years, up to 30–40% of weight lost is lean skeletal muscle, accelerating sarcopenia. GI adverse effects cause dehydration and postural syncope.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Reduce dose or deprescribe if progressive physical frailty or dizziness occurs.

---

### Class 24: Ophthalmic Eye Drops (Systemic Effects) — [3 Drugs]
1. Timolol Eye Drops (*Timolet, Glucomol*)
2. Betaxolol Eye Drops (*Betoptic*)
3. Pilocarpine Eye Drops (*Pilocar*)
* **Hidden Fall Mechanism:** Timolol is absorbed into systemic circulation via the nasolacrimal duct, causing severe **bradycardia ($<50\text{ bpm}$), heart block, and fainting**. Pilocarpine constricts pupils, destroying night vision.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:** Teach nasolacrimal occlusion (pinch inner eye corner for 2 minutes); consider laser trabeculoplasty (SLT) to deprescribe eye drops.

---

### Class 25: PDE-5 Inhibitors & Miscellaneous — [3 Drugs]
1. Sildenafil (*Silagra*)
2. Tadalafil (*Megalis*)
3. Teriparatide Injections (*Forteo, Bonista*)
* **Hidden Fall Mechanism:** Sildenafil/Tadalafil cause severe synergistic hypotension with nitrates or alpha-blockers. Teriparatide triggers acute orthostatic hypotension within 1–4 hours of injection.
* **Deprescribing / Administration Protocol:** Absolute contraindication of PDE-5 inhibitors with nitrates. Teriparatide must be injected while seated/supine, capped at 24 months lifetime use.

## 5. High-Risk Prescribing Traps Specific to India

1. **The "Chakkar" (Dizziness) Prescribing Cascade:** Older patients presenting with non-specific dizziness are routinely prescribed **Prochlorperazine (*Stemetil*) or Cinnarizine (*Stugeron*)** for months. This blocks cerebral dopamine receptors, inducing severe **Drug-Induced Parkinsonism (DIP), rigidity, and falls**.
2. **The "Pan-L" (Levosulpiride) Trap:** Prescribing PPI + Levosulpiride combinations for dyspepsia causes insidious Parkinsonian tremors, shuffling gait, and loss of postural reflexes in older adults.
3. **Triple Antihypertensive FDCs in Hot Climates:** Formulations combining **Telmisartan + Amlodipine + Chlorthalidone** lead to profound dehydration and orthostatic blackout when rising from bed or low seating.
4. **Sulfonylurea Hypoglycaemia:** Widespread use of long-acting **Glimepiride (*Amaryl*)** in frail elders causes recurrent, unrecognised neuroglycopenic falls.
5. **Ergonomic Hazards:** Combining vasodilators/diuretics with **Indian-style squatting toilets** triggers acute venous pooling and cerebral ischaemia upon standing up.
