# Clinical Practice Manual: Deprescribing Across All Medical Conditions & Complex Multimorbidity (2026 Reference Compendium)

**Scope:** Evidence-based clinical framework for doctors, geriatricians, and clinical pharmacists to assess, prioritize, and deprescribe Fall-Risk-Increasing Drugs (FRIDs) and high-risk medications across all major clinical specialties, organ systems, and disease combinations.  
**Consensus Alignment:** Western Australia Centre for Health and Ageing / UWA 2025 Guidelines (*Deprescribing in Older People*), World Falls Guidelines (WFG), STOPP/START Criteria Version 3, American Geriatrics Society (AGS) Beers Criteria, and ICMR-AIIMS National Geriatric Clinical Guidelines.

## 1. The 6-Step Universal Deprescribing Algorithm

Every deprescribing intervention must follow a structured clinical pathway to avoid rebound exacerbations, withdrawal syndromes, or patient loss of confidence:

```
[ Step 1: Detect Triggers ] 
  ├── Falls / Near-misses within 12 months
  ├── Postural drop (SBP >= 20 mmHg / DBP >= 10 mmHg)
  ├── Unexplained confusion, daytime drowsiness, or TUG > 12s
  └── Polypharmacy (>= 5 drugs) or Hyperpolypharmacy (>= 10 drugs)
          │
          ▼
[ Step 2: Comprehensive Medication Reconciliation (CMR) ]
  ├── Audit all Rx, Over-the-Counter (OTC), FDCs, herbal/ayurvedic meds
  ├── Match every drug to an active, validated pathology
  └── Calculate cumulative Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden (ACB) & Drug Burden Index (DBI)
          │
          ▼
[ Step 3: Stratify by Life Expectancy & Time-to-Benefit (TTB) ]
  ├── Evaluate Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS 1-9)
  ├── Balance: Will the patient live long enough to benefit from tight targets?
  └── Identify "Preventative" vs "Symptomatic" vs "Prognostic" drugs
          │
          ▼
[ Step 4: Prioritize Deprescribing Targets (One Drug at a Time) ]
  ├── Priority 1: Zero current indication or active adverse drug reaction (ADR)
  ├── Priority 2: High-risk FRID with safe alternative (e.g., Sulphonylurea -> DPP-4i)
  └── Priority 3: Preventative drug whose TTB exceeds clinical life expectancy
          │
          ▼
[ Step 5: Formulate the Tapering & Withdrawal Protocol ]
  ├── Dose de-escalation: 10% to 50% decrements every 1 to 4 weeks
  ├── Establish non-pharmacological support (CBT-I, physiotherapy, diet)
  └── Educate patient and caregiver on anticipated rebound vs true relapse
          │
          ▼
[ Step 6: Monitor, Re-evaluate & Document ]
  ├── Review vitals, labs, or symptoms at 1, 2, 4, and 12 weeks
  └── Document clinical justification in medical records and update pharmacy
```

## 2. Condition-by-Condition Deprescribing Protocols

### 2.1 Cardiovascular & Hemodynamic Disorders

#### A. Hypertension in the Older / Frail Adult
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Supine and 1- and 3-minute standing BP.
  - Presence of post-prandial hypotension (BP drops 30–60 minutes after meals).
  - Number of antihypertensive classes (e.g., ACEi + CCB + Thiazide + $\beta$-blocker).
* **Clinical Decision Rules:**
  - **If Supine SBP $< 120\text{ mmHg}$ or Standing SBP $< 110\text{ mmHg}$:** Over-treated. Deprescribe immediately.
  - **If Orthostatic Hypotension present ($\Delta\text{SBP} \ge 20\text{ mmHg}$):** Identify and stop vasodilating agents.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. *Step 1:* Stop non-cardioprotective peripheral $\alpha$-1 blockers (**Prazosin, Doxazosin, Terazosin**).
  2. *Step 2:* Reduce or eliminate centrally acting agents (**Clonidine, Methyldopa**)—taper slowly to avoid rebound hypertensive crisis.
  3. *Step 3:* If on combination therapy, halve the calcium channel blocker (**Amlodipine**) or stop the thiazide diuretic (**Chlorthalidone/Hydrochlorothiazide**).
  4. *Target Relaxation:* Aim for SBP **$135\text{--}145\text{ mmHg}$** and DBP **$75\text{--}85\text{ mmHg}$** in patients $\ge 75$ years or CFS $\ge 5$.

#### B. Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF)
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT) adherence: ARNI/ACEi, Beta-Blocker, MRA, SGLT2i.
  - Daily dry weight, jugular venous pulse (JVP), bilateral ankle edema, lung crackles.
* **Clinical Decision Rules:**
  - GDMT drugs reduce mortality and should be **retained** whenever hemodynamically tolerated.
  - **Loop Diuretics (Furosemide, Torsemide)** do *not* reduce mortality; they only control fluid volume. Excessive doses cause hypovolemia, pre-renal azotemia, orthostatic falls, and severe hypokalemia.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. If patient has no peripheral edema and clear chest, reduce Loop Diuretic dose by **$25\text{--}50\%$**.
  2. Transition to **flexible/PRN diuretic dosing**: Patient takes an extra tablet only if morning weight increases by $> 1.5\text{ kg}$ over 48 hours.
  3. Shift diuretic administration strictly to **morning ($< 10\text{ AM}$)** to prevent nocturia-related nighttime falls.

#### C. Arrhythmias & Atrial Fibrillation
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Resting ventricular rate; presence of resting bradycardia ($< 55\text{ bpm}$) or PR prolongation/heart block.
  - Serum Digoxin concentration (toxic threshold $> 0.9\text{ ng/mL}$ in older adults).
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. **Digoxin:** Deprescribe unless used as last resort for AF rate control. If eGFR $< 50\text{ mL/min}$, reduce dose to $0.0625\text{ mg}$ every other day or cease entirely.
  2. **Beta-blockers (Metoprolol, Atenolol):** If prescribed solely for sinus tachycardia without heart failure or post-MI ($> 12\text{ months}$), taper by $25\%$ every week to prevent rebound tachycardia.
  3. **Antiarrhythmics (Amiodarone):** Audit for pulmonary toxicity, ataxia, thyroid dysfunction, and QT prolongation; deprescribe in favor of rate-control where feasible.

### 2.2 Endocrine, Metabolic & Bone Diseases

#### A. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Current HbA1c, fasting and postprandial capillary blood glucose logs.
  - Episodes of neuroglycopenic symptoms (dizziness, diaphoresis, confusion, morning headaches).
  - eGFR (renal clearance of active sulfonylurea metabolites).
* **Clinical Decision Rules:**
  - Strict glycemic control ($\text{HbA1c} < 6.5\text{--}7.0\%$) carries a **3- to 5-fold higher risk of fatal and non-fatal falls**.
  - Microvascular protection takes $\ge 8\text{--}10\text{ years}$ to show clinical benefit.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. **Discontinue High-Potency Sulfonylureas:**
     - Cease **Glibenclamide** immediately (contraindicated in older adults).
     - Taper and stop **Glimepiride** and **Gliclazide**; switch to renal-safe DPP-4 inhibitors (**Linagliptin 5 mg daily**, requires no renal dose titration).
  2. **De-escalate Complex Insulin Regimens:**
     - Stop pre-mixed biphasic insulins and rapid-acting sliding scales.
     - Consolidate to once-daily basal insulin (Glargine or Degludec), titrating down to keep fasting glucose between **$120\text{--}160\text{ mg/dL}$**.
  3. **Target HbA1c Adjustments:**
     - Healthy/Fit: $7.0\%\text{--}7.5\%$
     - Moderate Frailty / Multiple Chronic Conditions: **$7.5\%\text{--}8.0\%$**
     - Severe Frailty / Dementia / Life Expectancy $< 5\text{ years}$: **$8.0\%\text{--}8.5\%$** (focus on avoiding symptomatic hyperglycemia $> 250\text{ mg/dL}$).

#### B. Dyslipidemia & Primary Prevention
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Indication: Primary prevention (no prior MI/stroke) vs. Secondary prevention (documented CAD, CABG, stroke).
  - Presence of statin-associated muscle symptoms (SAMS)—symmetrical proximal muscle weakness, myalgia, difficulty rising from low chairs.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. **Primary Prevention in Patients $\ge 75$ Years or CFS $\ge 5$:** Discontinue **Atorvastatin / Rosuvastatin**; Time-to-Benefit for primary prevention is $> 5$ years.
  2. **Secondary Prevention in Severe Frailty / Palliative Care:** If patient has muscle weakness impairing ambulation, reduce high-intensity statin (e.g., Atorvastatin 80 mg) to moderate/low dose (10–20 mg) or stop entirely if life expectancy is $< 12$ months.

#### C. Osteoporosis & Bone Mineral Management
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Duration of Oral Bisphosphonates (**Alendronate, Risedronate**).
  - Renal function (eGFR $< 30\text{--}35\text{ mL/min}$).
  - Atypical femoral fracture (AFF) and osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ) risk.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. **Bisphosphonate "Drug Holiday":** After **3 to 5 years** of oral bisphosphonates (or 3 years of IV Zoledronic acid), evaluate DEXA scan. If T-score is $>-2.5$ and no new fractures, initiate a **2- to 3-year drug holiday**. Bisphosphonates remain bound to bone matrix and retain anti-fracture efficacy for years.
  2. **Teriparatide (PTH Analogue):** Max treatment duration is **24 months**. Check for orthostatic hypotension within 4 hours of injection; if falls occur post-dose, administer injection strictly while patient is supine in bed.

### 2.3 Neurological & Neurodegenerative Disorders

#### A. Parkinson’s Disease (PD)
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Orthostatic vital signs (neurogenic orthostatic hypotension is common in PD and exacerbated by dopaminergic drugs).
  - Visual hallucinations, vivid dreams, daytime sleepiness (sleep attacks).
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. *Step 1:* Stop **Anticholinergic antiparkinsonians (Trihexyphenidyl, Benztropine)**—high risk of severe delirium and cognitive worsening.
  2. *Step 2:* Deprescribe or reduce **Dopamine Agonists (Pramipexole, Ropinirole)**—significant cause of orthostatic hypotension and impulse control disorders.
  3. *Step 3:* Deprescribe **COMT inhibitors (Entacapone)** or **MAO-B inhibitors (Rasagiline, Selegiline)** if dyskinesias or orthostasis persist.
  4. *Core Anchor:* Maintain **Levodopa/Carbidopa** as the cleanest, most effective agent, using lower, more frequent dosing intervals.

#### B. Epilepsy & Anticonvulsants
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Date of last unprovoked seizure; active seizure type.
  - Serum sodium level (hyponatremia risk with Carbamazepine/Oxcarbazepine).
  - Cerebellar signs: Nystagmus, finger-to-nose dysmetria, broad-based ataxic gait (Phenytoin, Carbamazepine toxicity).
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. If seizure-free for **$\ge 2$ years** with normal EEG and no structural epileptogenic lesion, consider a slow taper under neurology supervision.
  2. If used for **Neuropathic Pain** rather than epilepsy:
     - Taper **Pregabalin / Gabapentin** by $25\%$ every 1 to 2 weeks.
     - Adjust doses for renal clearance (Gabapentin accumulates rapidly when eGFR $< 60$).

#### C. Cognitive Decline & Dementia (Cholinesterase Inhibitors)
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Rate of cognitive decline; baseline pulse rate.
  - Presence of bradycardia ($< 55\text{ bpm}$), syncope, syncope-related falls, or severe GI intolerance (Donepezil, Rivastigmine).
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. If patient develops syncope, unexplained falls, or bradycardia, deprescribe **Donepezil / Rivastigmine**.
  2. In advanced dementia (FAST stage 7, bedbound, non-verbal), deprescribe **Memantine** and Cholinesterase Inhibitors (no meaningful functional preservation remains).
  3. Taper Donepezil from $10\text{ mg} \rightarrow 5\text{ mg}$ for 4 weeks, then discontinue.

### 2.4 Psychiatric, Affective & Sleep Disorders

#### A. Insomnia & Chronic Sedative-Hypnotic Use
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Duration of Benzodiazepine or Z-drug therapy.
  - Daytime residual "hangover" effect, morning ataxia, TUG test performance.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. **Long-Term Benzodiazepines (Alprazolam, Clonazepam, Lorazepam):**
     - Never stop abruptly (prevents seizures, severe rebound anxiety, and autonomic hyperarousal).
     - **Taper Rate:** Reduce total daily dose by **$10\text{--}25\%$ every $2\text{--}4\text{ weeks}$**.
     - For short-acting agents with high peak-trough swings (Alprazolam), switch to an equivalent dose of a longer half-life agent (Diazepam or Clonazepam) before completing the final taper steps.
  2. **Z-Drugs (Zolpidem, Zopiclone):**
     - Step down: Daily $\rightarrow$ Alternate nights for 2 weeks $\rightarrow$ Twice weekly for 2 weeks $\rightarrow$ Cease.
  3. **Non-Pharmacological Replacement:** Institute Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I), strict wake-up times, morning sunlight exposure, and stimulus control.

#### B. Major Depressive Disorder & Anxiety
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Episode duration and severity; time in full clinical remission ($> 6\text{--}12\text{ months}$).
  - Serum sodium (SIADH / hyponatremia screening for SSRIs/SNRIs).
  - Anticholinergic and cardiac effects (prolonged QTc, orthostasis with TCAs like Amitriptyline).
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. **Discontinue TCAs (Amitriptyline, Imipramine):** Switch to safer modern antidepressants if depression is active, or taper off over 4–6 weeks.
  2. **Tapering SSRIs/SNRIs (Sertraline, Escitalopram, Duloxetine):**
     - Reduce dose by **$25\text{--}50\%$ every 2 to 4 weeks**.
     - Watch for Antidepressant Discontinuation Syndrome (flu-like symptoms, "brain zaps", dizziness, insomnia); if symptoms arise, revert to prior dose and taper in smaller increments ($10\%$).

#### C. Behavioral & Psychological Symptoms of Dementia (BPSD)
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Prior documentation of psychosis, severe aggression, or immediate physical danger to self/others.
  - Time since the acute crisis resolved (guidelines recommend trial deprescribing after **$6\text{--}12\text{ weeks}$** of stability).
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. Taper atypical antipsychotics (**Quetiapine, Risperidone, Olanzapine**) by **$25\text{--}50\%$ every 2 weeks**.
  2. Implement the **DICE framework** (Describe, Investigate, Create, Evaluate) targeting environmental triggers, unrecognized physical pain, or constipation.

### 2.5 Renal, Fluid & Urological Disorders

#### A. Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD Stages 3–5)
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Serial eGFR, Serum Creatinine, and Serum Potassium ($\text{K}^+$).
  - Drug clearance: Water-soluble medications excreted renally.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. **Adjust / Deprescribe Renally Cleared FRIDs:**
     - **Gabapentin:** Max $100\text{--}300\text{ mg/day}$ if eGFR $< 30$; stop if sedation/ataxia occurs.
     - **Pregabalin:** Reduce by $50\text{--}75\%$.
     - **Allopurinol:** Reduce dose to match eGFR to prevent CNS neurotoxicity.
     - **Spironolactone / Eplerenone:** Stop or halve if $\text{K}^+ > 5.0\text{ mEq/L}$ or eGFR $< 30$.
  2. **SGLT2 Inhibitors (Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin):** While renal-protective, evaluate for volume depletion, fungal infections, and postural hypotension in frail patients with low BMI ($< 20$).

#### B. Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH) & Overactive Bladder (OAB)
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS), post-void residual volume, nocturia frequency.
  - Lying/standing blood pressure.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. **Non-selective $\alpha$-blockers:** Cease Prazosin or Terazosin; replace with uroselective **Silodosin (4–8 mg)** or **Tamsulosin (0.4 mg)** taken post-dinner.
  2. **Anticholinergic Bladder Antispasmodics (Oxybutynin, Tolterodine, Solifenacin):**
     - Stop immediately in patients with cognitive impairment, dry mouth, constipation, or recurrent falls.
     - Replace with lifestyle modifications (fluid restriction after 7 PM, pelvic floor muscle training) or a selective $\beta$-3 adrenergic agonist (**Mirabegron 25–50 mg**), monitoring blood pressure.

### 2.6 Gastrointestinal & Hepatic Conditions

#### A. Long-Term Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI) Use
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Indication audit: Was it prescribed for short-term dyspepsia, uncomplicated GERD, or non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) co-prescription that has now ended?
  - Absolute indications for lifelong therapy: Barrett’s esophagus, severe erosive esophagitis (LA Grade C/D), Zollinger-Ellison syndrome, or chronic antiplatelet + anticoagulant dual therapy with prior GI bleed.
* **Deprescribing Protocol (Step-Down Algorithm):**
  1. *Week 1–2:* Halve the current dose (e.g., Pantoprazole 40 mg $\rightarrow$ 20 mg once daily, or Rabeprazole 20 mg $\rightarrow$ 10 mg once daily).
  2. *Week 3–4:* Switch to alternate-day dosing (e.g., Pantoprazole 20 mg every other day).
  3. *Week 5 Onward:* Discontinue daily therapy. Transition to **PRN / On-Demand** dosing (take one tablet only when symptoms arise, max 3 days in a row) or use an $\text{H}_2$-receptor antagonist (**Famotidine 20 mg PRN**) or oral alginate antacid for rebound acid hypersecretion.

#### B. Fixed-Dose Combination Gastroprokinetics (India Context)
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Patient taking combinations such as **Pantoprazole + Levosulpiride (Pan-L)** or **Pantoprazole + Domperidone (Pan-D)**.
  - Presence of drug-induced parkinsonism: pill-rolling hand tremor, facial masking, bradykinesia, gait freezing.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. **Discontinue the FDC immediately.**
  2. Switch to plain, single-entity PPI if acid suppression is clinically needed.
  3. Educate the patient that tremors and rigidity may take **4 to 12 weeks** to fully resolve after Levosulpiride clearance.

#### C. Chronic Laxative Overuse
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Chronic stimulant laxative use (**Bisacodyl, Senna**).
  - Serum electrolytes: Hypokalemia and volume contraction.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. Taper stimulant laxatives; transition to osmotic agents (**Polyethylene Glycol / Macrogol** or **Lactulose**).
  2. Optimize dietary soluble fiber, hydration, and physical mobility.

### 2.7 Musculoskeletal, Spine & Chronic Pain Syndromes

#### A. Chronic Osteoarthritis & Back Pain
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Pain score (0–10 Numeric Rating Scale) vs. functional mobility vs. drug-induced sedation.
  - Co-prescriptions: Opioid + Gabapentinoid + Benzodiazepine (the "triple-sedation triad").
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. **Skeletal Muscle Relaxants:** Immediately discontinue **Thiocolchicoside, Chlorzoxazone, Metaxalone, Baclofen**. They offer no benefit in chronic musculoskeletal pain and cause profound dizziness and ataxia.
  2. **Opioids (Tramadol, Tapentadol, Morphine, Buprenorphine patch):**
     - Taper by **$10\text{--}20\%$ of original dose every 1 to 2 weeks**.
     - Avoid abrupt discontinuation to prevent opioid withdrawal syndrome (sweating, agitation, hyperalgesia, diarrhea).
  3. **First-Line Non-FRID Replacements:**
     - Topical NSAIDs (Diclofenac 1.16% gel applied 3–4 times daily; negligible systemic absorption, zero fall risk).
     - Scheduled Paracetamol ($650\text{ mg}$ TID, max $2\text{--}3\text{ g/day}$).
     - Supervised physical therapy and hydrotherapy for joint strengthening.

### 2.8 Respiratory & Pulmonary Disease

#### A. Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) & Asthma
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Systemic corticosteroid exposure (recurrent bursts of oral Prednisolone $\rightarrow$ steroid myopathy, proximal muscle weakness, osteoporosis).
  - Inappropriate sedative prescription: Benzodiazepines or opioids prescribed for dyspnea anxiety (risk of acute hypercapnic respiratory failure and nocturnal hypoxia).
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. **Oral Corticosteroids:** Taper oral Prednisolone to zero whenever acute exacerbation resolves. Maximize inhaled therapy (triple therapy: LAMA + LABA + ICS) to eliminate oral steroid dependence.
  2. **Sedatives:** Deprescribe sedatives; treat refractory breathlessness in end-stage COPD using low-dose, carefully titrated immediate-release morphine under specialist palliative guidance rather than benzodiazepines.
  3. **Theophylline / Aminophylline:** Deprescribe due to narrow therapeutic index, tremor, tachyarrhythmias, and seizure risk in older adults.

### 2.9 Ophthalmic & Visual Impairment

#### A. Glaucoma & Ophthalmic Drops
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Topical eye drop regimen.
  - Presence of systemic $\beta$-blockade symptoms: Resting bradycardia, fatigue, bronchospasm, and orthostatic falls in patients on **Timolol 0.5% drops**.
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. In patients with falls, syncope, or heart block, deprescribe topical **Timolol**.
  2. Switch to topical Prostaglandin analogues (**Latanoprost, Bimatoprost**) or topical Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors (**Dorzolamide**), which do not alter systemic hemodynamics.
  3. Teach nasolacrimal occlusion (punctal occlusion: pressing inner eyelid for 2 minutes after instillation) to minimize systemic vascular absorption.

### 2.10 Oncology, Advanced Frailty & Palliative Care

#### A. Severely Frail (CFS 7–8) & Palliative Patients
* **What Doctors Check:**
  - Goals of care: Longevity vs. Functional independence vs. Comfort and symptom relief.
  - Swallowing capability and pill burden fatigue ($> 10\text{ pills/day}$).
* **Deprescribing Protocol:**
  1. **Immediate Cessation:** Stop all primary prevention statins, antiplatelets, multivitamins, calcium/vitamin D, and bisphosphonates.
  2. **De-escalate Chronic Disease Targets:**
     - Cease blood pressure medications unless SBP $> 160\text{--}180\text{ mmHg}$ causes headache/distress.
     - Cease routine fingerstick glucose checks; adjust insulin/oral agents solely to prevent symptomatic hyperosmolar states (target BG $150\text{--}250\text{ mg/dL}$).
  3. **Preserve Strictly Comfort Drugs:** Analgesics, antiemetics, antisecretory agents, and low-dose anxiolytics titrated for dyspnea or acute distress.

## 3. Complex Multimorbidity Archetypes (Real-World Case Blueprints)

### Case Blueprint 1: The "Triad of Instability"
* **Patient:** 78-year-old male with CKD Stage 4 (eGFR 24), Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF), Type 2 Diabetes, and Osteoarthritis.
* **Current Regimen (11 Drugs):**
  1. Telmisartan 40 mg
  2. Chlorthalidone 12.5 mg
  3. Torsemide 20 mg
  4. Glimepiride 2 mg
  5. Metformin 500 mg
  6. Pregabalin 75 mg BD
  7. Tramadol 50 mg BD
  8. Pantoprazole + Levosulpiride (Pan-L)
  9. Atorvastatin 40 mg
  10. Calcium Carbonate + Vit D3
  11. Alprazolam 0.5 mg HS
* **Clinical Audit & Action:**
  - *Immediate Fall Triggers:* Glimepiride (severe hypoglycaemia risk in CKD), Torsemide + Chlorthalidone (double-diuretic volume contraction + hyponatremia), Pan-L (Levosulpiride parkinsonism), Tramadol + Pregabalin + Alprazolam (triple CNS sedation).
  - **Sequential Deprescribing Plan:**
    1. **Week 1:** Stop Glimepiride (switch to Linagliptin 5 mg); stop Pan-L (switch to plain Pantoprazole 20 mg); stop Chlorthalidone (rely on Torsemide alone).
    2. **Week 2–3:** Halve Alprazolam to 0.25 mg HS; halve Tramadol to 50 mg OD; adjust Pregabalin to 25 mg OD (renal dose for eGFR 24).
    3. **Week 4–6:** Discontinue Alprazolam and Tramadol; step down Pantoprazole to alternate days.
    4. **Result:** Pill burden cut from 11 to 5; fall risk reduced by $> 70\%$; renal parameters stabilized.

### Case Blueprint 2: The "Dementia with Agitation & Nocturia" Patient
* **Patient:** 82-year-old female with Alzheimer’s Disease (MMSE 14), Urge Incontinence, Hypertension, and Chronic Insomnia.
* **Current Regimen (7 Drugs):**
  1. Donepezil 10 mg
  2. Quetiapine 50 mg BD
  3. Zolpidem 10 mg HS
  4. Oxybutynin 5 mg BD
  5. Amlodipine 10 mg
  6. Amitriptyline 25 mg HS
  7. Cetirizine 10 mg PRN
* **Clinical Audit & Action:**
  - *Pathology of Conflict:* Severe Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden (ACB score = 8; Amitriptyline + Oxybutynin + Cetirizine + Quetiapine cancel out Donepezil's mechanism and accelerate memory loss while causing marked daytime drowsiness and ataxia).
  - **Sequential Deprescribing Plan:**
    1. **Week 1:** Cease Cetirizine and Amitriptyline immediately; taper Quetiapine to 25 mg HS.
    2. **Week 2–4:** Discontinue Oxybutynin (replace with scheduled timed voiding every 2.5 hours); taper Zolpidem to 5 mg for 10 days, then stop.
    3. **Week 5–8:** Taper Quetiapine to zero using behavioral de-escalation; reduce Amlodipine to 5 mg if standing SBP $< 130\text{ mmHg}$.
    4. **Result:** Resolution of delirium, cessation of nightly falls, improvement in alertness.

## 4. Master Tapering, Washout & Withdrawal Management Matrix

| Medication Class | Initial Taper Step | Taper Interval | Anticipated Withdrawal / Rebound Symptoms | Management Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Benzodiazepines** | Reduce by $15\text{--}25\%$ | Every $2\text{--}4\text{ weeks}$ | Rebound anxiety, insomnia, diaphoresis, tremor, irritability | Slow taper rate; switch to long-acting agent (Clonazepam/Diazepam); CBT-I. |
| **Z-Drugs (Zolpidem)** | Reduce dose by $50\%$ or alternate nights | Every $1\text{--}2\text{ weeks}$ | Sleep-onset latency rebound, vivid dreams | Reassure patient rebound lasts 3–5 days; sleep hygiene; warm milk/melatonin. |
| **Antidepressants (SSRIs/SNRIs)** | Reduce dose by $25\text{--}50\%$ | Every $2\text{--}4\text{ weeks}$ | "Brain zaps", nausea, dizziness, paresthesias, crying spells | Reinstate previous dose for 1 week, then taper by $10\%$ decrements using liquid formulation. |
| **Antipsychotics** | Reduce dose by $25\%$ | Every $2\text{ weeks}$ | Rebound agitation, motor restlessness (akathisia) | Check for physical discomfort (constipation, pain); behavioral management (DICE). |
| **$\beta$-Blockers** | Reduce dose by $25\text{--}50\%$ | Every $1\text{--}2\text{ weeks}$ | Rebound tachycardia, palpitations, transient hypertension | Monitor resting pulse; ensure HR $< 90\text{ bpm}$; avoid abrupt stopping in CAD. |
| **$\alpha$-Blockers (Prazosin)** | Reduce dose by $50\%$ | Every $1\text{ week}$ | Mild reflex hypertension, urinary hesitancy | Monitor BP; switch to uroselective agent (Silodosin) if urinary flow drops. |
| **Proton Pump Inhibitors** | Reduce by $50\% \rightarrow$ Alternate days | Every $2\text{ weeks}$ | Rebound acid hypersecretion (heartburn, dyspepsia) | Explain rebound is temporary acid up-regulation; use Famotidine or antacids PRN. |
| **Opioids** | Reduce dose by $10\text{--}20\%$ | Every $1\text{--}2\text{ weeks}$ | Restlessness, lacrimation, rhinorrhea, loose stools, aches | Slow taper; use Paracetamol, topical NSAIDs; Clonidine for autonomic signs. |
| **Skeletal Muscle Relaxants** | Discontinue or halve for 3 days | Immediate or 3 days | Mild muscle stiffness | Reassure; prescribe gentle stretching, heat packs, topical counter-irritants. |
| **Sulfonylureas** | Discontinue or halve | Immediate / 48 hrs | Hyperglycemia ($> 200\text{ mg/dL}$) | Replace with DPP-4i (Linagliptin); monitor fasting and 2-hr postprandial glucose. |

## 5. Bedside Clinical Audit Checklist & Communication Scripts

### The 5-Minute Bedside Clinical Audit Checklist
- [ ] **1. Vital Signs:** Supine BP, Standing BP at 1 min and 3 min. (Is SBP dropping $\ge 20$ or DBP $\ge 10$?)
- [ ] **2. Pulse Check:** Is resting pulse $< 60\text{ bpm}$ on rate-controlling drugs?
- [ ] **3. Mobility Check:** Can the patient stand from a chair without using arms? TUG test $> 12$ seconds?
- [ ] **4. Indication Check:** Does every active medication have a documented, current diagnostic justification?
- [ ] **5. High-Risk Class Screen:** Is the patient taking any of the 25 FRID classes?
- [ ] **6. FDC Screen:** Is there a hidden dopamine antagonist (Levosulpiride, Domperidone) in the GI prescription?
- [ ] **7. Lab Audit:** eGFR, Serum Sodium ($Na^+$), Serum Potassium ($K^+$), Serum Magnesium ($Mg^{2+}$), HbA1c.
- [ ] **8. Goal Alignment:** Does the patient’s life expectancy match the time-to-benefit of all preventative medications?

### Physician-Patient Communication Script (Overcoming Deprescribing Hesitancy)

> **Doctor:** *"Mr./Mrs. [Patient Name], as we grow older, our liver and kidneys process medicines differently, and our blood vessels become less elastic. Some medicines you started years ago were very helpful then, but right now, they are actually working against you—they are making you lightheaded when you stand up and making your legs unsteady.*
> 
> *Our biggest goal is to keep you walking independently and out of the emergency room. We are not taking away medicines that protect your heart or keep you comfortable. Instead, we are going to slowly step down [Drug Name] over the next few weeks.*
> 
> *We will not stop it suddenly. We will lower the dose gently, monitor how you feel, and if any uncomfortable symptoms return, we have safe alternatives ready. You are in complete control of this process."*
