# Complete Listing of Fall-Risk-Increasing Drugs (FRIDs) & Deprescribing Guidance

**Reference Guideline:** *Deprescribing in Older People: A clinical practice guideline* (16 September 2025)  
**Publishing Body:** Western Australia Centre for Health and Ageing (WACHA) & Centre for Optimisation of Medicines, The University of Western Australia (UWA)  
**Target Population:** Older adults aged $\ge 65$ years ($\ge 50$ years for Aboriginal & Torres Strait Islander peoples).

* **Non-Dihydropyridine Calcium Channel Blockers (Cardiac) (2):**
  20. Diltiazem *(PBS top 100)*
  21. Verapamil *(PBS top 100)*
* **Beta-Adrenoreceptor Blockers (5):**
  22. Atenolol *(PBS top 100)*
  23. Bisoprolol *(PBS top 100)*
  24. Carvedilol
  25. Metoprolol (succinate & tartrate) *(PBS top 100)*
  26. Nebivolol *(PBS top 100)*
  *(Note: Propranolol is also listed; Sotalol is catalogued under antiarrhythmics)*
* **Centrally Acting Antiadrenergics (3):**
  27. Clonidine
  28. Methyldopa
  29. Moxonidine *(PBS top 100)*
* **Direct Arteriolar Vasodilators (2):**
  30. Hydralazine
  31. Minoxidil

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Exaggerated arterial blood pressure reduction, blunted baroreceptor sensitivity, cerebral hypoperfusion, and postural/postprandial hypotension (affecting up to 20% of older adults). Beta-blockers cause fatigue, blunted chronotropic response, and bradycardia.

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* **Deprescribing Trigger:** Offer deprescribing when $\text{SBP} < 150\text{ mmHg}$ in patients aged $\ge 80$ years (or $< 140\text{ mmHg}$ aged 75–79) with **recurrent falls, orthostatic hypotension, or frailty**.
* **Withdrawal Sequence:** Deprescribe centrally acting agents first $\rightarrow$ Beta-blockers $\rightarrow$ CCBs $\rightarrow$ ACEi/ARBs last.
* **Tapering Protocol:** Halve dose every 2 weeks for beta-blockers and centrally acting agents (clonidine, moxonidine) to avoid rebound hypertensive crisis and tachycardia.
* **Restart Parameters:** Restart if $\text{SBP} \ge 150\text{ mmHg}$ or $\text{DBP} \ge 90\text{ mmHg}$ on 3 consecutive readings; restart immediately if $\text{SBP} \ge 180$ or $\text{DBP} \ge 110\text{ mmHg}$.

### Class 2: Diuretics — [9 Drugs]
*(Source: pp. 101–107)*

#### Complete Drug List:
* **Loop Diuretics (2):**
  1. Furosemide *(PBS top 100)*
  2. Bumetanide
* **Thiazide Diuretics (1):**
  3. Hydrochlorothiazide *(PBS top 100)*
* **Thiazide-Like Diuretics (2):**
  4. Chlortalidone
  5. Indapamide *(PBS top 100)*
* **Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists / K-Sparing (3):**
  6. Spironolactone *(PBS top 100)*
  7. Eplerenone
  8. Finerenone
* **Epithelial Sodium Channel Blocker (1):**
  9. Amiloride

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Extracellular fluid depletion, acute volume contraction, postural hypotension, severe hyponatraemia, hypokalaemia, muscle weakness, and urgent nighttime micturition (precipitating falls in the dark).

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* Deprescribe when prescribed for dependent ankle oedema (prescribing cascade from CCBs) or when used solely for hypertension.
* **Furosemide Tapering Protocol:**
  * $< 40\text{ mg/day}$: Discontinue without tapering.
  * $40\text{ mg/day}$: Halve dose ($20\text{ mg}$) for 1 week, then cease completely.
  * $80\text{ mg/day}$: Halve dose ($40\text{ mg}$) for 2 weeks, then cease completely.
  * $80\text{--}160\text{ mg/day}$: Reduce by $40\text{ mg}$ every 2 weeks until reaching $40\text{ mg}$, then follow above.
* **Monitoring:** Weekly weight monitoring, blood pressure, serum potassium, and renal function (eGFR, creatinine).

### Class 3: Organic Nitrates — [3 Drugs]
*(Source: pp. 84–86)*

#### Complete Drug List:
1. Glyceryl trinitrate (GTN)\*
2. Isosorbide mononitrate\*
3. Isosorbide dinitrate

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Venodilation causes extensive peripheral blood pooling, reducing venous return and cardiac output, precipitating orthostatic hypotension, light-headedness, and syncope.

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* Deprescribe long-acting nitrates in stable coronary artery disease if angina-free and no PRN sublingual nitrates have been required for $\ge 6$ months.
* **Tapering:** Gradually reduce oral dose (e.g., $120\text{ mg} \rightarrow 60\text{ mg} \rightarrow 30\text{ mg} \rightarrow \text{stop}$) over several weeks.
* **Safety Rule:** Always provide and educate on short-acting sublingual GTN for acute relief if needed.

### Class 4: Antiarrhythmics & Digitalis Glycosides — [2 Drugs]
*(Source: pp. 79–83)*

#### Complete Drug List:
1. Digoxin\*
2. Sotalol\*

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Narrow therapeutic index; age-related decline in renal clearance leads to drug accumulation, visual disturbances ("yellow halos"), nausea, profound bradycardia, high-grade AV block, and collapse.

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* **Digoxin:** Offer deprescribing in HFrEF patients who are in sinus rhythm and stabilised on modern 4-pillar GDMT; cease abruptly if serum level is subtherapeutic ($<1.2\text{ ng/mL}$).
* **Sotalol:** Deprescribe if permanent atrial fibrillation is established (rhythm restoration abandoned) or if sinus rhythm has been stable for $\ge 2\text{--}3$ months. Taper by 25% every 1–2 weeks to prevent rebound ventricular arrhythmias.

### Class 5: Urological Alpha-1 Adrenoceptor Antagonists (BPH) — [3 Drugs]
*(Source: pp. 124–126)*

#### Complete Drug List:
1. Tamsulosin\* *(and combined with Dutasteride\*)*
2. Alfuzosin
3. Silodosin

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Vascular smooth muscle relaxation lowers systemic peripheral resistance, inducing significant postural hypotension, dizziness, and syncope.

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* Deprescribe in men whose symptoms have resolved post-prostatectomy or TURP, or in those experiencing symptomatic orthostatic hypotension or falls.
* Can be ceased without tapering. (Advise patients to keep a permanent record of past use prior to cataract surgery due to the risk of Intraoperative Floppy Iris Syndrome).

### Class 6: Antipsychotics — [19 Drugs]
*(Source: pp. 178–188)*

#### Complete Drug List:
1. Amisulpride
2. Aripiprazole
3. Asenapine
4. Brexpiprazole
5. Cariprazine
6. Chlorpromazine
7. Clozapine
8. Droperidol
9. Flupentixol
10. Haloperidol
11. Lurasidone
12. Olanzapine
13. Paliperidone
14. Periciazine
15. Quetiapine
16. Risperidone
17. Trifluoperazine
18. Ziprasidone
19. Zuclopenthixol

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Central sedation, blockade of alpha-1 adrenoceptors causing orthostatic hypotension, dopamine D2 antagonism causing Parkinsonism, shuffling gait, bradykinesia, rigidity, and acute dystonic reactions.

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* **Dementia BPSD Trigger:** Review at **12 weeks**. Deprescribe if symptoms are stable, resolved, or if there was no initial response within 2 weeks.
* **Fall Criterion:** Recurrent falls is an explicit clinical indication that harms outweigh benefits.
* **Tapering Protocol:** Standard reduction is 25% every 1–4 weeks. In chronic or high-dose therapy, adopt **hyperbolic tapering** (progressively smaller reductions of 5–10% every 4+ weeks) down to micro-doses to prevent withdrawal dyskinesia and behavioural rebound.

### Class 7: Benzodiazepine Anxiolytics — [6 Drugs]
*(Source: pp. 189–196)*

#### Complete Drug List:
1. Alprazolam
2. Bromazepam
3. Clobazam
4. Diazepam\*
5. Lorazepam
6. Oxazepam

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Enhanced GABAergic inhibitory neurotransmission causes sedation, cerebellar psychomotor impairment, prolonged reaction time, and peripheral muscle relaxation.

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* Deprescribe all long-term use ($> 4$ weeks).
* **Tapering Protocol:** Reduce by 25% every 1–4 weeks. Patient educational brochures (EMPOWER model) markedly increase cessation success.
* **Monitoring:** Differentiate withdrawal anxiety (peaks within 1–2 weeks) from chronic recurrence.

* **Z-Drugs / Non-Benzodiazepine Receptor Agonists (2):**

*(Source: pp. 164–168)*

#### Complete Drug List:
1. Alfentanil
2. Buprenorphine\*
3. Codeine *(and with paracetamol\*)*

7. Morphine
8. Oxycodone\* *(and with naloxone\*)*
9. Pethidine
10. Remifentanil
11. Tapentadol\*

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Central mu-opioid activation induces somnolence, cognitive slowing, orthostatic hypotension, dizziness, and uncoordinated motor execution.

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* **Recurrent falls** is an explicit clinical trigger indicating that harms outweigh benefits in chronic non-cancer pain.
* **Tapering Protocols:**
  * Used $< 12$ months: Taper by 5–10% morphine equivalent dose (MED) **weekly**.
  * Used $\ge 12$ months: Taper by 5–10% MED **monthly**.
  * Deprescribe long-acting formulations first. Caution patients regarding rapid loss of tolerance.

### Class 11: Gabapentinoids — [2 Drugs]
*(Source: pp. 164, 172–173)*

#### Complete Drug List:
1. Pregabalin\*
2. Gabapentin

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Modulation of alpha-2-delta voltage-dependent calcium channels produces significant somnolence, dizziness, peripheral oedema, and cerebellar gait ataxia.

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* Deprescribe if no clinically demonstrable pain relief or functional improvement is achieved after 2–3 months of treatment for neuropathic pain or sciatica.
* Taper dose by 25% weekly.

### Class 13: Sedating Antiemetics / Dopamine Antagonists — [2 Drugs]
*(Source: pp. 51–52)*

#### Complete Drug List:
1. Prochlorperazine\*
2. Metoclopramide *(cross-noted on p. 51)*

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Dopamine D2 antagonism and anticholinergic properties induce sedation, orthostatic hypotension, extrapyramidal movement disorders, and acute dystonic reactions.

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* Restrict to acute vertigo/nausea for the shortest duration (hours to days).
* Contraindicated in Parkinson’s disease.
* Deprescribe prescribing cascades (e.g., initiated for diuretic- or CCB-induced dizziness). Taper from TID $\rightarrow$ BID $\rightarrow$ Daily $\rightarrow$ Cease every 1–2 weeks.

### Class 14: Genitourinary Anticholinergics — [5 Drugs]
*(Source: pp. 122–123)*

#### Complete Drug List:
1. Oxybutynin
2. Tolterodine
3. Solifenacin
4. Darifenacin
5. Propantheline

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Antimuscarinic burden impairs central cognitive function, causes acute delirium, visual accommodation paresis (blurred vision), and nighttime disorientation.

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* **Explicit Deprescribing Trigger:** Offer deprescribing to older adults with dementia, cognitive impairment, or a **high risk of falls**.
* Deprescribe anticholinergic prescribing cascades (e.g., initiated for incontinence induced by cholinesterase inhibitors).
* Cease without tapering (or halve dose for high-dose regimens).

### Class 15: Hypoglycaemia-Inducing Diabetes Agents — [5+ Drugs]
*(Source: pp. 55–62)*

#### Complete Drug List:
* **Sulfonylureas (4):**
  1. Gliclazide\*
  2. Glibenclamide
  3. Glimepiride
  4. Glipizide
* **Insulins (Class):**
  5. Insulin glargine\*, aspart, lispro, detemir, degludec, isophane/NPH

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Severe neuroglycopenia ($\text{glucose} < 4.0\text{ mmol/L}$) leads to acute confusion, weakness, diaphoresis, visual blurring, loss of consciousness, and falls.

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* Deprescribe or de-intensify **insulin first, then sulfonylureas** whenever the two most recent consecutive $\text{HbA}_{1c}$ readings are below individualised targets ($< 7.0\text{--}7.5\%$ for robust; $< 8.0\%$ for complex/frail).
* Oral sulfonylureas can be discontinued abruptly without tapering; insulins require cautious down-titration.

### Class 16: Dopaminergic Anti-Parkinson Agents — [1 Core Drug]
*(Source: pp. 175–177)*

#### Complete Drug List:
1. Levodopa (formulated with benserazide, carbidopa\*, and entacapone)

#### Fall-Risk Pathophysiology:
Causes orthostatic hypotension in $> 50\%$ of patients, along with motor dyskinesias, fluctuating "off" immobility, and sudden daytime somnolence.

#### New Guideline Deprescribing Directives:
* Deprescribe or reduce dose if non-motor side effects (intractable orthostasis, hallucinations, severe sedation) outweigh motor benefits.
* Taper gradually by 100 mg (or 1 tablet) every 2 weeks to avoid acute akinetic crisis or neuroleptic malignant-like syndrome.

### Special Additional Fall-Risk Warning: High-Dose Bolus Vitamin D
*(Source: pp. 148, 150)*
* The guideline explicitly highlights that large, intermittent bolus doses of vitamin D (**$> 60,000\text{ IU}$ monthly or $> 1,000\text{--}4,000\text{ IU}$ daily**) are paradoxically associated with an **increased incidence of falls** in older adults.
* Routine supplementation should be deprescribed in community-dwelling older adults whose serum $25(\text{OH})\text{D} \ge 50\text{ nmol/L}$.

## 8. "Hidden" & Indirect Fall-Risk-Increasing Drugs (FRIDs)

Beyond the classical sedative and antihypertensive classes, the following medication groups act as **Indirect FRIDs** by inducing muscle wasting (sarcopenia), dehydration, severe weakness, blurred vision, or drug-induced Parkinsonism.

### Summary Table of Hidden / Indirect FRIDs

| Class | Individual Generic Drugs | Primary Fall-Inducing Mechanism | Clinical Guidance & Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Proton-Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)** | Pantoprazole, Rabeprazole, Omeprazole, Esomeprazole, Lansoprazole | Hypomagnesaemia (muscle tremors/cramps), B12 deficiency (sensory ataxia), bone demineralisation | Deprescribe after 8–12 weeks; taper by 50% every 2 weeks. Beware of Indian FDCs with Levosulpiride (causes Drug-Induced Parkinsonism). |
| **SGLT2 Inhibitors (Gliflozins)** | Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin, Canagliflozin | Osmotic diuresis, hypovolaemia, postural hypotension, loss of lean skeletal muscle mass | Deprescribe if causing recurrent orthostatic dizziness, dehydration, or progressive muscle wasting/frailty. |
| **Skeletal Muscle Relaxants** | Thiocolchicoside, Tizanidine, Baclofen, Chlorzoxazone | Marked skeletal hypotonia (flaccid leg weakness), central sedation, loss of protective reflexes | Discontinue long-term use; strictly avoid combining with NSAIDs or sedatives in older adults. |
| **Systemic Corticosteroids** | Prednisolone, Methylprednisolone, Dexamethasone | Steroid myopathy (atrophy of pelvic girdle and thigh muscles), adrenal insufficiency weakness | Taper dose by 10–20% weekly; when reaching 5 mg prednisolone equivalent, taper very slowly by 1 mg every 4–8 weeks. |
| **GLP-1 Receptor Agonists** | Semaglutide, Liraglutide, Dulaglutide | Accelerated lean muscle mass loss (sarcopenia), dehydration from GI adverse events | Monitor muscle strength; reduce dose or deprescribe if causing physical frailty or postural instability. |
| **Ophthalmic Eye Drops** | Timolol, Betaxolol, Pilocarpine | Systemic beta-blocker absorption (severe bradycardia, heart block, syncope); miotics cause night vision impairment | Reassess necessity; teach nasolacrimal duct occlusion (pressing inner eye corner for 2 minutes); consider SLT laser. |
| **PDE-5 Inhibitors** | Sildenafil, Tadalafil | Potent systemic vasodilation; synergistic hypotension with nitrates or alpha-blockers | Absolute contraindication with nitrates; separate from alpha-blockers by at least 24–48 hours to avoid syncopal collapse. |
| **Bone Anabolic PTH Analogue** | Teriparatide | Transient post-injection orthostatic hypotension and lightheadedness within 1–4 hours | Cap therapy at 24 months; administer only while sitting or lying down; transition sequentially to bisphosphonates. |
| **Chronic Stimulant Laxatives** | Bisacodyl, Senna, Sodium Picosulfate | Hypokalaemia (skeletal muscle weakness), dehydration, precipitate nighttime rushing to the toilet | Taper off daily use; transition to dietary hydration, fibre, and PRN gentle osmotic laxatives. |
